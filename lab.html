How the Lab works

  • Each nanosecond, every data qubit picks up phase δω·dt, where δω is the noise's frequency shift on that qubit (steady or OU-fluctuating, scaled by its envelope).
  • The stabilizer “looks at” qubit q at the end of its CNOT. Phase picked up after that moment counts towards the next round.
  • When the readout ends, each qubit's looked-at phase θ becomes a full Z flip with probability sin²(θ/2), and the tilt is erased.
  • The syndrome is the parity of the four qubits: two flips cancel, so a burst that flips two qubits in the same round can go unseen.
  • A detection event fires when the syndrome changes. Its answer reaches the electronics after the readout plus the electronics delay. Latency is measured from the noise start time to that moment.
  • Simplification: each data qubit is treated separately. This gives the correct timing, while a real surface code keeps the qubits in a shared entangled state.
